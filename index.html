What makes a successful fusion?

  1. The first gene supplies the start codon. Cut before its stop codon so translation can continue.
  2. The second gene supplies the final stop codon. Its own start codon is usually removed from this internal position.
  3. Check the reading frame across the entire join. A linker base can complete a partial junction codon, but the second protein must return to its original frame.
  4. Keep the highlighted motifs intact. A continuous ORF alone does not guarantee that the desired protein parts remain.
  5. If you add a linker, inspect the stop markers below its sequence and check the translated fusion.

This simplified exercise joins coding DNA directly. Real fusion design also requires checks of protein folding, localization, and function.
